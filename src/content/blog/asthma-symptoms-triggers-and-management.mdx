## Quick Answer

Asthma causes episodes of wheezing, cough, breathlessness, or chest tightness because the airways become inflamed and narrow. Common triggers include dust, smoke, pollution, pollen, mould, infections, cold air, exercise, and strong smells. Asthma is manageable: the right inhaler plan, correct technique, trigger reduction, and regular review can prevent attacks and protect daily activity.

## Key Takeaways

- Asthma symptoms can come and go; feeling well does not always mean airway inflammation has disappeared.
- Wheezing, cough, chest tightness, and breathlessness should be assessed when they recur.
- Controller inhalers reduce airway inflammation; reliever inhalers provide quick symptom relief but should not be the only long-term strategy.
- Smoke, indoor dust, pollution, respiratory infections, and workplace exposures are common triggers in India.
- Severe breathing difficulty, blue lips, confusion, or poor response to the reliever is an emergency.

## What Are the Symptoms of Asthma?

Asthma symptoms vary from person to person and can change from one day to the next. The most common symptoms are wheezing, a dry or irritating cough, shortness of breath, and chest tightness. Some people mainly cough, while others notice difficulty breathing during exercise, at night, or after a cold.

Symptoms may be worse at night or early in the morning. They can also appear after laughing, running, entering a dusty room, inhaling smoke, or being exposed to cold air. Symptoms that repeatedly disturb sleep, limit walking, or require frequent reliever use deserve a medical review.

Not every cough or wheeze is asthma. Chest infection, allergic rhinitis, acid reflux, heart disease, vocal cord problems, and other lung conditions can look similar. A clinician may need to examine you and arrange breathing tests before confirming the diagnosis.

## What Triggers Asthma in India?

Triggers are individual, but the following are common:

| Trigger | Where it may occur | Practical step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dust and dust mites | Bedding, carpets, soft toys, stored clothes | Wash bedding regularly, reduce clutter, and ventilate rooms |
| Smoke | Cigarettes, incense, mosquito coils, cooking fumes | Keep the home smoke-free and improve kitchen ventilation |
| Air pollution | Traffic, construction, seasonal smog, indoor burning | Check local air quality and limit strenuous outdoor activity during severe pollution |
| Pollen and mould | Gardens, damp walls, monsoon humidity | Reduce dampness and avoid known high-exposure areas |
| Infections | Colds, flu, COVID-19, and other respiratory illnesses | Discuss vaccinations and early treatment with your clinician |
| Cold air or exercise | Winter mornings, running, sudden exertion | Warm up and follow your personalised exercise plan |

Perfumes, room fresheners, paint fumes, cleaning products, and workplace chemicals can also trigger symptoms. Do not assume every exposure must be eliminated; identify patterns and make practical changes that are safe and sustainable.

## How Is Asthma Diagnosed?

The diagnosis is based on a pattern of variable symptoms plus evidence that airflow changes over time. Your clinician may ask when symptoms occur, whether they wake you, what triggers them, and whether you have allergies or a family history of asthma.

Breathing tests such as spirometry can help show obstruction and improvement after a bronchodilator. Peak-flow monitoring may also be useful for some patients. A chest X-ray or other test may be considered when the symptoms are unusual, severe, or not responding as expected.

Do not diagnose yourself from an inhaler response alone. A temporary improvement can occur in several conditions, and repeated cough may need a different treatment.

## What Is the Difference Between Controller and Reliever Inhalers?

Controller treatment reduces airway inflammation and lowers the risk of future symptoms and attacks. It is usually taken regularly according to the prescribed plan, even when you feel well. Some modern treatment plans combine an anti-inflammatory medicine with a reliever effect, but the exact inhaler and dose must be selected by a clinician.

Reliever treatment is used for quick symptom relief. Needing it frequently, waking at night, or finding that it no longer gives the usual relief can indicate poor control. Do not increase the dose, share an inhaler, or stop controller treatment without medical advice.

Inhaler technique matters. Ask your doctor, nurse, or pharmacist to watch you use the device. A spacer can improve delivery for many metered-dose inhalers, particularly in children, older adults, and people who find coordination difficult. Keep track of doses and check expiry dates.

## What Should I Do During Worsening Symptoms?

Keep a written asthma action plan with your medicines, usual symptoms, warning signs, and the number to call for help. When symptoms begin, sit upright, remain as calm as possible, and follow the reliever instructions in that plan. Avoid lying flat if it makes breathing harder.

Seek urgent medical help if:

- Breathing is rapidly worsening or the person cannot speak in full sentences.
- The lips or face look blue, grey, or unusually pale.
- The person is drowsy, confused, exhausted, or struggling to stay awake.
- The prescribed reliever is not working as expected or symptoms return quickly.
- There is severe chest pain, fainting, or another concerning symptom.

Do not rely on steam, honey, herbal mixtures, or a nebuliser at home as a substitute for urgent assessment. These may delay treatment while the attack becomes more serious.

## How Can I Reduce Asthma Attacks?

Use prescribed controller treatment consistently and attend reviews even when symptoms are quiet. Keep a diary of symptoms, night waking, exercise limitation, reliever use, infections, weather, pollution, and suspected exposures. This helps distinguish a trigger from a coincidence.

Keep bedrooms free from smoke and strong fragrances. Wash bedding as advised, address dampness and mould, and avoid burning incense or mosquito coils indoors if they worsen symptoms. If cooking fumes trigger attacks, improve ventilation and discuss safer options for the household.

People with asthma should not automatically avoid exercise. Walking, cycling, swimming, and other activities can support fitness. Warm up gradually and discuss symptoms that repeatedly occur during exercise; your treatment plan may need adjustment.

If allergies, nasal congestion, reflux, obesity, anxiety, or sleep problems are affecting control, address them with your clinician. Good asthma management is often broader than changing one inhaler.

## What If Asthma Symptoms Happen at Work or School?

Repeated cough, wheezing, or chest tightness during a particular job, shift, classroom, or workshop may indicate an occupational or environmental exposure. Flour, cleaning chemicals, paints, dust, textile fibres, laboratory materials, and mould can all be relevant. Note when symptoms begin, whether they improve on days away, and which materials are present.

Do not stop work or remove a child from school without discussing the pattern with a clinician. A proper assessment may include breathing tests before and after work, a review of safety equipment, and advice about ventilation or exposure control. Keep prescribed medicines accessible and make sure trusted adults know what to do during worsening symptoms.

## How Are Asthma and Allergies Connected?

Allergic rhinitis, eczema, and asthma can occur together because they involve related airway and immune responses. A blocked or inflamed nose can make breathing feel worse and may disturb sleep. Treating nasal symptoms with the medicines recommended by a clinician can improve comfort, but nasal sprays or antihistamines should not replace asthma controller treatment when it is indicated.

Avoid testing yourself with suspected allergens at home. Food allergy is different from ordinary food intolerance, and unnecessary food restriction can cause nutritional problems. If a food, medicine, insect sting, or exposure causes rapid swelling, widespread hives, vomiting, dizziness, or breathing difficulty, seek emergency care because this may be a severe allergic reaction.

## How Often Should Asthma Be Reviewed?

Review asthma after a new diagnosis, after an attack, when reliever use increases, or when symptoms affect sleep and activity. At review, ask your clinician to check inhaler technique, adherence, trigger exposure, lung function, vaccination needs, and whether the action plan is still appropriate.

Good control is not only the absence of today's symptoms. It also means reducing the risk of future attacks and avoiding unnecessary treatment. Never feel embarrassed about asking for an inhaler demonstration; small technique errors can prevent medicine from reaching the lungs.

Keep an updated list of medicines and allergies when travelling, and carry enough prescribed inhaler treatment for the whole trip. Weather changes, airport smoke, dust, and respiratory infections can disrupt control. Plan ahead with your clinician if you are travelling far from medical care or have previously needed emergency treatment.

## The Bottom Line

Asthma is a manageable airway condition, not a reason to accept ongoing breathlessness or repeated emergency visits. Recognise your symptoms, learn the difference between controller and reliever treatment, use inhalers correctly, reduce personal triggers, and keep an action plan. Get urgent help when severe symptoms do not improve promptly.
