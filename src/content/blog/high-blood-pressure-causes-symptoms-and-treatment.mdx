**High blood pressure causes and treatment** start with understanding one simple fact: your heart is working too hard. Blood pressure is the force of blood against your artery walls, and when it stays elevated at 130/80 mmHg or above, you have hypertension. This condition damages your heart, brain, kidneys, and eyes over time — but it is highly manageable with the right medical care and lifestyle changes.

## Key Takeaways

- High blood pressure affects nearly 1 in 3 Indian adults, and most don't know they have it until complications arise.
- The leading causes include excess salt, abdominal obesity, stress, family history, and diabetes — not just ageing.
- Most people have **no symptoms** until BP is dangerously elevated; regular checking is the only way to know.
- Stage 1 hypertension (130-139/80-89 mmHg) often responds to lifestyle changes alone, but medication may be needed.
- Natural approaches like reducing salt, walking daily, and eating potassium-rich Indian foods can lower BP by 5-10 mmHg.
- Untreated hypertension leads to heart attack, stroke, kidney failure, and vision loss — early treatment prevents these.

## What Causes High Blood Pressure?

In my clinic, I often see patients who are shocked by a high reading because they "feel fine." Most cases — about 90% — are **primary (essential) hypertension**, meaning no single medical cause is found. However, several factors clearly drive it up.

### Common Causes You Can Control

- **Excess salt intake**: Most Indians consume 8-10 grams of salt daily, double the WHO recommendation of 5 grams. Salt makes your body retain water, raising blood volume and pressure.
- **Abdominal obesity**: Fat around your belly presses on kidneys and releases hormones that constrict blood vessels.
- **Physical inactivity**: A sedentary lifestyle weakens your heart's efficiency, raising resting blood pressure.
- **Chronic stress**: Stress hormones like cortisol and adrenaline temporarily spike BP; when stress is constant, the effect becomes permanent.
- **Alcohol and smoking**: Both damage artery walls and stiffen blood vessels.
- **Poor sleep**: Less than 6 hours of sleep per night is linked to higher BP due to disrupted circadian rhythms.

### Causes You Cannot Control

- **Family history**: Genetics account for 30-50% of hypertension risk. If a parent had high BP, your risk doubles.
- **Age**: Blood vessels naturally stiffen with age; systolic BP rises steadily after age 40.
- **Gender**: Men under 55 have higher risk; women catch up after menopause.

### Medical Conditions That Cause Secondary Hypertension

About 10% of cases stem from another condition — **secondary hypertension**. These include chronic kidney disease, thyroid disorders, sleep apnea, and hormonal conditions like Cushing's syndrome. Certain medications — painkillers (NSAIDs), steroids, and some decongestants — also raise BP. If your BP stays high despite three medications, I evaluate for these underlying causes.

## What Are the Symptoms of Hypertension?

Most people with high BP have **zero symptoms** — this is why hypertension is called the **"silent killer."** You can have BP of 180/110 mmHg and feel perfectly normal. However, some individuals do experience symptoms, especially when BP spikes suddenly.

### Symptoms to Watch For

- **Morning headaches** — usually at the back of the head, dull and throbbing
- **Dizziness or light-headedness**
- **Nosebleeds** (epistaxis), especially recurrent
- **Blurred vision** or seeing spots
- **Palpitations** — feeling your heart racing or pounding
- **Fatigue or confusion** during high-stress periods
- **Chest pain or shortness of breath** — this is a red flag, seek emergency care

### When Symptoms Signal Danger

A **hypertensive crisis** occurs when BP exceeds 180/120 mmHg. If accompanied by chest pain, difficulty speaking, weakness on one side, or vision loss, it indicates end-organ damage — stroke, heart attack, or kidney failure. This is a medical emergency. In my emergency shifts, I've seen young patients with stroke from undiagnosed hypertension; these cases are preventable.

## How Do I Lower Blood Pressure Naturally?

Lifestyle modification is the cornerstone of hypertension management. For stage 1 hypertension without other risk factors, doctors often prescribe 3-6 months of lifestyle changes before starting medication. These natural methods work — and they work faster than most people expect.

### 1. Cut Salt — The #1 Change

Aim for less than 5 grams of salt daily (about one teaspoon). Indian cuisine is salt-heavy, so here are practical tips:

- Cook with **rock salt (sendha namak)** in moderation — it's still sodium chloride.
- Rinse pickles, papads, and salted snacks before eating.
- Use lemon, amla, coriander, and mint to flavour food instead of salt.
- Avoid processed foods: bread, namkeen, instant noodles, and restaurant food hide massive amounts of sodium.
- Check labels — anything with more than 400 mg sodium per serving is high.

### 2. Walk Every Single Day

Brisk walking for 30-40 minutes daily, five days a week, can reduce systolic BP by 5-8 mmHg. This equals the effect of a low-dose medication. Indian cities have morning and evening walk culture; use it. If you cannot do 30 minutes continuously, break it into three 10-minute sessions — the effect is similar.

### 3. Eat More Potassium and Magnesium

These minerals counter sodium's effects and relax blood vessel walls. Indian foods rich in potassium include:

| Food | Potassium Content | How to Add |
|------|-------------------|------------|
| Coconut water | 600 mg per glass | Morning drink |
| Banana | 422 mg per medium | Evening snack |
| Moong dal | 540 mg per cooked cup | Daily dal |
| Palak (spinach) | 840 mg per cooked cup | Subzi or salad |
| Sweet potato | 540 mg per medium | Roasted snack |
| Curd (dahi) | 380 mg per cup | With meals |

### 4. Manage Stress with Breathing

Chronic stress keeps cortisol levels high, damaging blood vessel walls. Practice **4-7-8 breathing**: inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 7, exhale for 8. Repeat five times, twice daily. In my practice, patients who do this report better sleep and calmer moods, which directly reflects in lower home BP readings.

### 5. Reduce Alcohol and Quit Smoking

Alcohol raises BP even in moderate amounts; limiting to 2 drinks per week or stopping entirely helps. Smoking causes immediate BP spikes and long-term arterial damage. Nicotine replacement therapy and behavioural counselling improve quit rates — ask your doctor for help.

### 6. Lose Weight If Needed

Excess weight is directly linked to BP. Each kilogram lost reduces BP by roughly 1 mmHg. For a patient overweight by 15 kg, weight loss of 5 kg can lower BP by 5 mmHg — that's enough to postpone or reduce medication.

## What Is Stage 1 Hypertension Treatment?

**Stage 1 hypertension** is defined as BP consistently at 130-139/80-89 mmHg. In India, adults with stage 1 hypertension and no other health issues (like diabetes, kidney disease, or heart conditions) usually start with lifestyle changes for 3-6 months.

### Treatment Approach by Risk Profile

| Patient Profile | Initial Treatment |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Stage 1, no other conditions | Lifestyle changes for 3-6 months |
| Stage 1 + diabetes or kidney disease | Lifestyle changes PLUS medication |
| Stage 1 + high cardiovascular risk (calculated by doctor) | Lifestyle changes PLUS medication |
| Stage 1 + home BP persistently high | Medication, often low-dose ACE inhibitor |

### Medications Commonly Used

- **ACE inhibitors** (Enalapril, Ramipril) — relax blood vessels; watch for dry cough
- **ARBs** (Telmisartan, Losartan) — similar action, fewer side effects
- **Calcium channel blockers** (Amlodipine) — especially effective in Indian patients with salt sensitivity
- **Diuretics** (Chlorothalidone) — remove excess fluid; useful with high salt intake

Most patients need only one medication initially. The goal is BP below 130/80 mmHg. If one drug fails, I add a second from a different class — combination therapy works better than doubling the dose. You should never stop medication on your own; rebound hypertension is dangerous.

## When Should I See a Doctor for High Blood Pressure?

If your home BP readings are consistently above 130/80 mmHg for one week, see a physician. You should also seek medical advice if you have:

- A family history of early heart disease or stroke
- Diabetes, kidney disease, or high cholesterol
- Recurrent headaches, nosebleeds, or dizziness
- Pregnancy or planning pregnancy
- Side effects from current BP medications

In India, hypertension often coexists with diabetes and high cholesterol — a combination we call the **"metabolic syndrome."** I recommend a complete check-up with blood tests, ECG, and urine analysis for anyone with persistent BP elevation. Your doctor will also assess for target organ damage to the eyes, kidneys, and heart.

Don't wait for symptoms. I've treated too many patients in their 40s who ignored elevated readings and now live with heart failure or post-stroke disability. Hypertension is treatable; the earlier you start, the better your heart, brain, and kidneys survive the decades ahead.

## How Is High Blood Pressure Diagnosed?

Diagnosis is based on **multiple readings** — never a single measurement. Your doctor may use:

- **Clinic BP measurements** — taken after 5 minutes of rest, with an appropriately sized cuff
- **Home BP monitoring** — 2 readings morning and evening for 7 days; average of these guides treatment
- **Ambulatory BP monitoring** — a 24-hour device; useful for "white coat" (clinic-only high BP) or "masked" (normal clinic, high home) hypertension

I also check for complications with blood tests (creatinine, electrolytes, blood sugar, lipid profile) and an ECG. An eye examination is essential — the retina shows early signs of hypertensive damage.

## Does High Blood Pressure Cause Headaches?

Yes, but only when BP is very high — typically above 180/110 mmHg. These headaches are often **bilateral and throbbing**, worse in the morning, and may accompany dizziness. However, most people with mild-to-moderate hypertension have no headaches at all. If you have chronic headaches and elevated BP, I evaluate for other causes like stress, dehydration, or medication side effects.

## Are There Home Remedies for High Blood Pressure in India?

Many Indian households have used traditional remedies for generations, and some have genuine scientific backing. Here are evidence-informed **high BP home remedies India** that work alongside — not instead of — medical treatment:

- **Garlic (lahsun)**: Raw garlic, 2-3 cloves daily, may lower systolic BP by 8-10 mmHg due to allicin. Crush it, wait 10 minutes, then consume to activate allicin.
- **Fenugreek seeds (methi)**: Soak 1 teaspoon overnight, eat in the morning. Methi contains galactomannan, which supports BP and blood sugar control.
- **Hibiscus tea (gudhal)**: Drinking 2 cups daily has shown reduction of 7-8 mmHg systolic. Steep dried flowers for 10 minutes.
- **Amla (Indian gooseberry)**: High in vitamin C, acts as an antioxidant that protects blood vessel lining. One raw amla daily or amla juice fresh.
- **Watermelon seeds**: Blend dried seeds into powder; take 1 teaspoon twice daily. Contains citrulline, which relaxes blood vessels.
- **Onion juice with honey**: Traditional recipe; 1 teaspoon onion juice + 1 teaspoon honey, morning on empty stomach — supports BP in early hypertension.

These remedies work best when your overall diet is low in salt and high in vegetables. I recommend using them as complementary to your prescribed treatment — never as a replacement for blood pressure medication.

## What If I Miss a Dose of BP Medication?

If you miss a dose, take it as soon as you remember — but skip it if it's almost time for your next dose. **Never double up** doses; this can cause dangerously low BP and dizziness, leading to falls. Missing a few doses won't cause immediate harm, but frequent misses lead to uncontrolled BP and damage over months. Use a daily pill organizer or set a phone alarm to build consistency.
